## TL;DR (inferred, low confidence)

The empirical foundation underneath [[fu2013meaning]]'s "near vs. far"
result. The title states the factorial structure directly: ideation
performance is measured as a function of three manipulated properties of
design examples — **analogical distance**, **commonness**, and
**modality** — and the framing ("benefits *and pitfalls*") signals that the
effect is non-monotonic rather than "more distant is better."

## Claims (inferred from title and citation context, NOT verified)

- Analogical distance, commonness, and modality of examples are each
  manipulated and each affect ideation outcomes — i.e. distance is not the
  only dimension that matters, which is a caution for any design that treats
  it as the sole knob.
- "Pitfalls" in the title, together with this literature's central concern
  with **design fixation**, strongly suggests documented *harms* from certain
  example types — plausibly that common examples induce fixation. Unverified.
- Outcomes are decomposed across ideation quantity, quality, novelty, and
  variety, per the standard measures of this subfield. Unverified.
