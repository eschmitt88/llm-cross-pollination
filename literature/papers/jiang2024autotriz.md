# AutoTRIZ: Automating Engineering Innovation with TRIZ and Large Language Models

## TL;DR

Automates the classic TRIZ (Theory of Inventive Problem Solving) four-step
pipeline — specific problem → general problem (contradiction) → general
solution (inventive principles) → specific solution — end to end with an
LLM plus a *fixed, external* knowledge base (39 engineering parameters,
the 39×39 contradiction matrix, 40 inventive principles, all sourced from
Altshuller's patent-derived TRIZ framework, not generated by the LLM).
This is essentially [[abstract-then-reinstantiate]] already built and
evaluated for engineering design — the closest existing system to this
project's target skill, minus the "foreign topic" randomness (TRIZ's
"foreign domain" is the fixed inventive-principles taxonomy itself, drawn
from cross-industry patent analysis).

## Claims

- The core TRIZ move — abstract a specific problem into a general
  contradiction, look up a general-purpose solution, then map back to a
  specific solution — can be fully automated by decomposing it into LLM
  modules interleaved with a non-LLM lookup step (the contradiction
  matrix), and this beats letting the LLM free-associate an ideation
  path.
- Randomness/inconsistency in contradiction identification is inherent
  and can be quantified with information entropy across repeated runs —
  directly reusable methodology for our own H1 randomness measurement.
- Knowledge should stay *external and fixed* (RAG-style retrieval from
  the real TRIZ tables) rather than trusting the LLM to recall correct
  TRIZ parameters/principles from pretraining — the paper frames this
  explicitly as retrieval-augmented generation solving a hallucination
  risk.

## Methods

Four-module pipeline, three driven by LLM calls (GPT-4, version
20231106), one by deterministic lookup:
- **Module 1** — `T = f1(U)`: extract/clean a clear problem statement
  from raw user input.
- **Module 2** — `C = {P_improve, P_worsen} = f2(T)`: identify the
  engineering contradiction as a pair from the 39 fixed engineering
  parameters (given to the LLM as in-context knowledge, e.g. `[INDEX] 1
  [TITLE] Weight of moving object [DESCRIPTION] ...`).
- **Module 3** — `I = f3(CM, C)`: **not an LLM call** — a deterministic
  lookup into the classic 39×39 contradiction matrix to retrieve the
  applicable inventive principles.
- **Module 4** — `S = f4(T, C, I)`: synthesize problem + contradiction +
  retrieved principles into a structured LaTeX solution report.

Randomness measurement (directly reusable for H1): for a fixed problem
statement, ran contradiction identification (Module 2) 100 times and
computed the **information entropy** of the resulting distribution over
parameter pairs (H(X) = −Σ P(xᵢ)log₂P(xᵢ), range 0–6.64 for their output
space). Lower entropy = higher self-consistency. They explicitly reject
self-consistency/majority-voting as inappropriate here, arguing (like
TRIZ practice) that a single problem legitimately has multiple valid
contradictions — this is a useful counter-argument to consider when
designing our own "is the LLM being appropriately random vs. just noisy"
diagnostics.

## Results

- Built a 10-case textbook benchmark (from 7 published TRIZ textbooks) as
  ground truth. 7/10 cases had AutoTRIZ's top-3 contradiction detections
  match or half-match the textbook's expert-identified contradiction.
- Case study: full BTMS (Battery Thermal Management System) design,
  evaluated against three quantitative engineering metrics, compared to
  a human-expert-generated benchmark solution.
- No comparison against a "just ask the LLM to brainstorm" baseline —
  the paper does not measure whether the TRIZ structure itself
  outperforms unstructured ideation; it only validates against human
  TRIZ-expert textbook analyses.

## Critique / open questions

Self-reported limitations (Section 7), several are exactly our project's
open questions: no objective mechanism to evaluate solution quality
(parallel to [[novelty-usefulness-tradeoff]]); single contradiction per
run, no multi-perspective/multi-seed exploration (parallel to H5,
explicitly flagged as future work); small case base (10 textbook cases,
"may introduce bias"). The fixed 40 inventive principles are themselves a
coarse, non-distance-controlled analogy frame (cf. [[sampling-frame]]).

## Follow-up

- **Relevance: 5** — Closest prior system to the project's Q2 design:
  TRIZ's four-step flow *is* [[abstract-then-reinstantiate]] already
  instantiated for engineering. The entropy-based randomness diagnostic
  (§4.2) should be adapted directly for H1. Also a live example of
  granularity choice (H4): TRIZ's inventive principles are method-level,
  not field-level, supporting the H4 hypothesis.
