## TL;DR (from established secondary understanding)

The founding statement of **conceptual blending** (conceptual integration).
Where [[gentner1983structuremapping]] models analogy as a directed *mapping*
that projects relational structure from a source onto a target, blending
theory models it as *integration*: two (or more) input mental spaces, plus
a **generic space** capturing what they share, project selectively into a
**blended space** that develops **emergent structure** — structure present
in neither input. The blend runs as its own conceptual entity.

## Claims (theory, not empirical findings)

- Meaning construction routinely involves integration networks over multiple
  mental spaces, not just two-place source→target mappings.
- The four-space architecture — two inputs, a generic space, a blend — with
  selective projection into the blend.
- **Emergent structure** in the blend is the theory's central move: the
  output can contain relations that neither input contained, arising through
  composition, completion, and elaboration ("running the blend").
- Blending is presented as a general, largely unconscious cognitive
  operation underlying metaphor, counterfactuals, grammar, and humour — not
  a special-purpose creativity mechanism.

## Methods

- Theoretical/analytical, built on worked linguistic examples. No experiment;
  this is a framework paper.

## Critique / open questions

- The standard critique in the literature is that blending theory is
  **post-hoc and unfalsifiable**: it can describe almost any creative output
  after the fact, but generates few advance predictions and no metric. That
  is a real limitation for a project that needs to *score* transfer.
  Gentner's structure-mapping, by contrast, yields testable predictions
  about which mappings are preferred (systematicity).
- "Emergent structure" is precisely the phenomenon this project wants but is
  also the least operationalized part of the theory.
