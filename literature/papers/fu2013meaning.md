## TL;DR (secondhand)

Randomly assigns designers "near" or "far" analogical stimuli — drawn from
a U.S. patent database structured by a latent-semantic-analysis + Bayesian
structural-form clustering method — while working an engineering design
problem, and measures the effect of stimulus distance on design output.
This is the paper [[analogical-distance]] and `docs/research-plan.md`
already cite by name as the source of the "near vs. far" design literature
motivating H3.

## Claims (secondhand, low confidence)

- "Near" and "far" are treated as relative, not absolute — distance is
  operationalized via structural clustering of the patent database, not a
  fixed taxonomy.
- Secondary summaries report a pattern consistent with H3's expected shape:
  near stimuli tend to improve feasibility/usefulness of design outputs,
  while far stimuli tend to improve novelty/uniqueness — with diminishing
  or reversed returns if stimuli are too distant.
- Won a Best Paper award per co-author Christian Schunn's CV, per web
  search — an independent signal of the work's standing in the design-by-
  analogy subfield even without a direct read of the paper.

## Methods (secondhand)

- Patent-database stimuli structured by LSA + Bayesian clustering into a
  distance metric; controlled experiment assigning near vs. far stimuli to
  designers on a fixed problem; design outputs rated for quality/novelty.
