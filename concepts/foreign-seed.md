# Foreign seed

## Definition

A topic from outside the problem's home field, chosen by a procedure the
model does not control, and injected into a query with the intent that a
*mechanism* from that topic transfers to the problem.

## Why it matters here

It is the unit of the whole project. A seed has three attributes the
experiments vary independently: its **source population**
([[sampling-frame]]), its **granularity** (field / subfield / named method —
hypothesis H4 says method-level transfers better), and its **distance** from
the problem ([[analogical-distance]]). How the seed is *used* is the second
half of the project ([[abstract-then-reinstantiate]], [[seed-brief-isolation]]).

## Connections

- Distinct from the model "being creative": a seed is an external
  constraint, the way a random word is in de Bono's lateral-thinking
  exercises or a card is in Oblique Strategies.
- The user's motivating example — organic chemistry → neural-network
  training — is a field-level seed; "retrosynthesis" or "protecting groups"
  would be the method-level version of the same seed.
- [[uzzi2013atypical]]'s bibliometric finding — the highest-impact papers
  combine a conventional core with an atypical fringe, not atypicality
  throughout — is the closest large-scale, real-world evidence that
  motivates *why* a foreign seed should be dosed and blended with the home
  field's own toolkit rather than substituting for it wholesale.
