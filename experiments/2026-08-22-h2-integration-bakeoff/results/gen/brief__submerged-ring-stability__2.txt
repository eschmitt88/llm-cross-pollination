# Salivary Gland Tumors: Diagnosis and Treatment — Technical Brief

Salivary gland neoplasms are pathologically heterogeneous (>20 WHO-recognized entities) but clinical management converges on a small set of recurring mechanisms and techniques: cytologic risk stratification, fusion-gene-driven molecular diagnosis, histologic grading, and margin/nerve-sparing surgical and radiotherapeutic decision-making.

1. **Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (MSRSGC)** — Fine-needle aspiration cytology is triaged into six categories (I: non-diagnostic, II: non-neoplastic, III: AUS, IVa: benign neoplasm, IVb: SUMP, V: suspicious for malignancy, VI: malignant), each carrying an empirically derived risk of malignancy (ROM). This lets the surgeon convert a cytologic read directly into a management pathway (observation vs. core biopsy vs. resection extent) without waiting for frozen section. Standard form: category-specific ROM ranges (e.g., ~5% for II, 60–90% for V, >90% for VI), reported per institution and used to set the threshold for proceeding to surgery.

2. **Recurrent fusion oncogenes as lineage- and entity-defining drivers** — Several salivary carcinomas are chromosomal-translocation diseases: *CRTC1(MECT1)–MAML2* in mucoepidermoid carcinoma (t(11;19)) constitutively activates Notch/CREB transcriptional output; *MYB–NFIB* (or *MYBL1–NFIB*) in adenoid cystic carcinoma dysregulates a MYB transcriptional program; *ETV6–NTRK3* in mammary analogue secretory carcinoma creates a constitutively active NTRK3 tyrosine kinase. These are detected by FISH break-apart probes or RT-PCR/NGS fusion panels and are used both diagnostically (distinguishing morphologic mimics) and, for *ETV6–NTRK3*, therapeutically (predicts response to TRK inhibitors).

3. **Perineural invasion / neurotropism in adenoid cystic carcinoma** — ACC preferentially tracks along nerve sheaths (perineural and, less often, intraneural spread), driven by tumor–nerve paracrine signaling (e.g., NGF/TrkA, GDNF/RET axes) that allows tumor cells to migrate substantial distances from the visible mass along named nerves (e.g., facial, lingual, inferior alveolar). This produces skip lesions beyond grossly clear margins, is a major cause of "positive margin despite adequate resection," and is the single strongest indication for postoperative radiotherapy to the nerve tract even after complete gross excision.

4. **Histologic grading of mucoepidermoid carcinoma (AFIP/modified Goode system)** — A point-based score assigns weighted points for cystic component <20% (2 pts), neural invasion (2), necrosis (3), mitoses ≥4/10 hpf (3), and anaplasia (4); points sum to a grade (low ≤4, intermediate 5–6, high ≥7). Grade correlates with 5-year survival (>90% low-grade vs. ~25–40% high-grade) and is the primary determinant of whether elective neck dissection and adjuvant radiotherapy are added to resection.

5. **Capsular pseudopodia and tumor spillage in pleomorphic adenoma** — The pseudocapsule of pleomorphic adenoma is frequently incomplete, with finger-like tumor extensions (pseudopodia) penetrating it; simple enucleation transects these extensions and seeds the field, producing multifocal recurrence years to decades later (recurrence risk with enucleation alone can exceed 20–45% vs. <2% with a margin of normal tissue). This mechanistic fact is why extracapsular dissection or superficial/partial parotidectomy with a cuff of normal parenchyma has replaced enucleation as the surgical standard, balanced against facial nerve preservation.

6. **Facial-nerve-sparing dissection using intraoperative nerve monitoring** — Because major salivary neoplasms (especially parotid) sit against or within the facial nerve plexus, resection proceeds via nerve identification (main trunk or retrograde branch approach) with continuous electromyographic monitoring (train-of-four/free-run EMG) that signals mechanical or thermal irritation before transection. The trade-off managed is oncologic completeness (wider margin, lower local recurrence) versus nerve morbidity (facial paresis/paralysis); monitoring shifts this trade-off favorably without changing the resection plane itself.

7. **Postoperative radiotherapy (PORT) indication and dosing** — PORT is added based on a composite risk score (high-grade histology, close/positive margins, perineural or lymphovascular invasion, T3–T4 stage, nodal disease, bone invasion), since these features predict local failure with surgery alone. Standard dosing is ~60–66 Gy in 2 Gy fractions to the tumor bed/nerve tract (with a boost to gross residual disease), or fast-neutron therapy at specialized centers for unresectable/recurrent ACC given its relative radioresistance to photons; the procedural logic is a linear accumulation of risk factors that crosses a threshold triggering adjuvant treatment rather than a single deterministic indication.
