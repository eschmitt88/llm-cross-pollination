# HIV/AIDS Research and Interventions: Technical Brief

HIV/AIDS science operates at the intersection of virology, pharmacology, and behavioral epidemiology. Effective practice requires reasoning simultaneously about within-host viral dynamics, population-level transmission, and the adherence/structural barriers that determine whether a biologically effective intervention actually reaches people. The seven mechanisms below are the core toolkit practitioners reach for when designing, delivering, or evaluating an intervention.

1. **Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) and Viral Suppression**
   ART combines drugs from multiple classes (NRTIs, NNRTIs, protease inhibitors, integrase inhibitors) that each block a distinct step of the HIV replication cycle, so that resistance mutations arising against one drug rarely confer cross-resistance to the others. Combining three active agents drives replication below the threshold at which resistant variants can be selected, suppressing plasma HIV RNA to <50 copies/mL ("undetectable"). Clinically the trade-off is potency versus tolerability/adherence burden, since sustained suppression — not peak potency — is what prevents both disease progression and transmission (per U=U, discussed below). The standard metric is time-to-suppression and viral load trajectory modeled by multi-phase exponential decay after treatment initiation.

2. **Viral Load and CD4 Count as Monitoring Biomarkers**
   Plasma HIV RNA (viral load, copies/mL, log₁₀-scaled) tracks active replication in real time, while CD4+ T-cell count (cells/mm³) tracks cumulative immune damage; the two decouple during early immune reconstitution, so both are followed jointly. Viral load rebound is the primary signal of treatment failure or non-adherence and triggers resistance testing before a regimen switch. CD4 count historically gated treatment eligibility and opportunistic-infection prophylaxis thresholds (e.g., <200 cells/mm³ for PCP prophylaxis), though WHO now recommends treat-all regardless of CD4.

3. **Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U) / Treatment as Prevention (TasP)**
   Sustained viral suppression reduces genital-tract viral shedding to the point that sexual transmission risk becomes effectively zero, a causal chain established by the HPTN 052 and PARTNER/PARTNER2 cohort studies (zero linked transmissions across thousands of condomless sex acts in serodifferent couples with a suppressed partner). This reframes ART as a prevention tool at the population level, not just an individual treatment, and is the biological basis for "treat-all" and rapid-ART-start policies. The operative quantity is time durably below the assay's detection limit, not a single test result.

4. **Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)**
   Oral PrEP (tenofovir/emtricitabine) or long-acting injectable cabotegravir pre-loads HIV-negative individuals' tissues with drug concentrations sufficient to abort infection at the point of exposure, before systemic seeding occurs. Efficacy is strongly adherence-dependent: tenofovir diphosphate concentration in dried blood spots (a validated adherence biomarker) correlates near-monotonically with protection, with near-complete efficacy at ≥4 doses/week for oral regimens. The core trade-off practitioners manage is real-world effectiveness versus trial efficacy, driven almost entirely by adherence rather than biological failure.

5. **Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT)**
   Transmission occurs in utero, intrapartum, or via breastfeeding; each route is interrupted by a distinct mechanism — maternal ART to suppress maternal viral load, infant post-exposure prophylaxis to cover residual peripartum exposure, and either suppression-covered breastfeeding or replacement feeding. Without intervention, cumulative transmission risk is ~15–45%; with combined maternal ART plus infant prophylaxis it falls below 2%. The procedural form is a time-gated cascade (antenatal → intrapartum → postpartum/infant) rather than a single dosing decision, since each stage independently gates residual risk.

6. **Adherence Measurement and Support**
   Because ART and PrEP efficacy are dose-dependent, adherence itself is treated as a measurable, modifiable clinical variable, not a background assumption. Standard measurement methods trade cost and bias differently: self-report (cheap, recall/social-desirability bias), pill counts and pharmacy refill data (proxy for dispensing, not ingestion), electronic pill-bottle monitoring (MEMS caps), and pharmacologic assays (drug-level in blood/hair, most objective but costly). Interventions (adherence counseling, differentiated service delivery, long-acting formulations) are evaluated against these measures because viral suppression is a downstream, lagging indicator of adherence failure.

7. **Stigma as a Structural Determinant of Cascade Attrition**
   HIV-related stigma operates at enacted (discrimination), internalized (self-devaluation), and anticipated (fear of future discrimination) levels, each acting as a distinct causal pathway that reduces testing uptake, delays care entry, and drives disengagement at specific points along the HIV care cascade (test → link to care → initiate ART → retain in care → suppress). Because the cascade is multiplicative — overall population-level viral suppression is the product of the proportion cleared at each stage — stigma-driven attrition at any single stage compounds into large losses in aggregate suppression, which is why stigma-reduction and status-neutral, differentiated service delivery models are treated as biomedical-adjacent interventions rather than purely social ones. Measurement typically uses validated multi-item stigma scales (e.g., internalized/anticipated/enacted subscales) correlated against cascade-stage retention.
