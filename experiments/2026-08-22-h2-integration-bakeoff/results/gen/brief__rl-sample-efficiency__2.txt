# Lymphoma Diagnosis and Treatment: Technical Brief

Lymphoid neoplasms are diagnosed by integrating morphology, immunophenotype, and increasingly genomics, and are treated with regimens whose selection depends on molecular subtype, disease burden, and response kinetics. The seven mechanisms below are the working toolkit a hematopathologist or oncologist applies from biopsy to survivorship.

1. **Cell-of-Origin (COO) Molecular Subclassification.** Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) arises from B cells arrested at distinct points of germinal-center transit, producing biologically and prognostically distinct germinal-center B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell (ABC) subtypes with different NF-κB dependency and chemosensitivity. Classification uses gene expression profiling (GEP, e.g., Lymph2Cx/Affymetrix-based linear predictor scores) or immunohistochemical surrogate algorithms (Hans classifier: CD10, BCL6, MUM1) that approximate the GEP call from paraffin sections.

2. **Immunohistochemical Panel-Based Lineage and Subtype Assignment.** Neoplastic lymphoid cells are typed by antigen expression detected via antibody-antigen binding with chromogenic or fluorescent readout, distinguishing B-cell (CD19, CD20, CD79a), T-cell (CD3, CD5), and Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg (CD15, CD30, weak/absent CD20) lineages. Marker combinations (e.g., cyclin D1 for mantle cell lymphoma, BCL2/BCL6/MYC for double/triple-expressor DLBCL) narrow differential diagnosis before or alongside molecular testing, trading the speed/cost of IHC against the resolution of genomic assays.

3. **Recurrent Cytogenetic Translocations and FISH Detection.** Chromosomal translocations juxtapose oncogenes with immunoglobulin enhancers, causing constitutive overexpression: t(14;18)(IGH-BCL2) in follicular lymphoma, t(11;14)(IGH-CCND1) in mantle cell lymphoma, and MYC rearrangements (often with BCL2/BCL6, "double/triple-hit") in aggressive DLBCL. Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) with break-apart or fusion probes detects these events on interphase nuclei, reported as percent of nuclei with an abnormal signal pattern against a validated cutoff.

4. **International Prognostic Index (IPI) and Risk Stratification.** Clinical prognosis is modeled as an additive risk score from independent variables — age >60, elevated LDH, ECOG performance status ≥2, Ann Arbor stage III/IV, and >1 extranodal site — each scored 0 or 1 and summed (0–5) to stratify low, low-intermediate, high-intermediate, and high-risk groups with distinct 5-year survival. Revised (R-IPI) and NCCN-IPI variants recalibrate cutpoints and weight age/LDH continuously to improve discrimination in the rituximab era.

5. **CHOP-Based Immunochemotherapy with Anti-CD20 Antibody.** Rituximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody against CD20, binds surface antigen on malignant B cells and triggers complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, depleting tumor B cells while sparing CD20-negative plasma cells and stem cells. Combined with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP, typically 6–8 cycles at 21-day intervals), it is the backbone for CD20+ B-cell lymphomas, with dose intensity and cycle count adjusted by stage and risk category.

6. **FDG-PET/CT for Staging and Interim Response Assessment.** Metabolically active lymphoma cells take up ¹⁸F-fluorodeoxyglucose at rates exceeding surrounding tissue due to elevated GLUT transporter expression and glycolytic flux, producing focal uptake quantified by standardized uptake value (SUV) relative to background. The Deauville 5-point scale compares lesion uptake to mediastinal blood pool and liver reference points at baseline and interim/end-of-treatment scans, guiding response-adapted therapy (e.g., escalation or de-escalation based on interim score).

7. **Minimal/Measurable Residual Disease (MRD) Monitoring via Clonal IG/TCR Rearrangement.** B- and T-cell receptor gene loci undergo somatic V(D)J recombination early in lymphocyte development, producing a junction sequence unique to each malignant clone that serves as a patient-specific molecular marker. PCR (consensus primers per BIOMED-2/EuroClonality protocols) or next-generation sequencing of this rearrangement detects residual clonal cells at sensitivities of 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁶, used to confirm remission depth and detect molecular relapse ahead of clinical recurrence.
